One Everett company's published guide breaks it down by home size instead: $150–$250 for a small home with up to 10 windows, $250–$425 for a medium home with 15 to 20 windows, and $425–$700 or more for a large home with 25 or more windows (Velocity Cleaning Systems, Everett pricing guide).

Nationally, Angi puts professional window cleaning at about $8–$16 per window for inside and out, or $4–$8 per window for one side only. Exterior-only cleaning typically runs 40–60% less than the same job with the inside included.

What moves the price

  • How many windows, and how big. More glass takes more time; per-window pricing adds up fast on a big house.
  • Stories and access. Second and third story windows, and glass that takes a ladder to reach, cost more than ground floor glass.
  • Inside, outside, or both. Inside work means moving carefully around your rooms; exterior-only is the cheaper option.
  • Condition. Heavy buildup, paint overspray or hard water spots take longer than a maintenance clean.
  • What is included. Frames, sills and screens are part of a standard One Way clean. Some companies charge extra for screens or tracks, so check what a quote actually covers.

Hard water removal

Hard water removal prices

Hard water removal is priced per window because the work depends on how much mineral buildup there is and whether it sits on the surface or has etched into the glass. Angi's cost data puts hard water stain removal at about $10–$30 per window, depending on the severity of the staining.

That range matches how the work goes. Surface spots polish off. Etched glass takes longer, and etching can be improved, not always fully restored. Any honest quote has to say which one you are dealing with, and you cannot always tell from a photo.

That is why every One Way hard water job starts with a test spot. One window gets cleaned first, you see the result, and the price for the rest is set with the result in front of both of us. If the test spot does not come clean, you will know before you have paid for the whole house.

What moves the price

  • How many windows are spotted. One shower-facing window is a small job; a whole sprinkler side of the house is not.
  • Surface spots vs etching. Surface mineral wipes and polishes off; etched glass needs more passes and still may not come back perfectly.
  • Access. Spotted glass on a second story takes ladder time on top of the polishing.

Pressure washing

Pressure washing prices

Pressure washing is usually priced by the square foot or by the surface. Angi's Seattle figures put it at about $0.10–$0.50 per square foot. A standard driveway runs about $100–$250, and a patio about $100–$200, depending on size and how much grime has built up.

Around here the driver is the wet winter. Concrete that looked fine in September can come out of February dark with film and algae, and the north side of a house stays dirty longer than the south side. Two driveways the same size can be very different jobs.

What moves the price

  • Square footage. A two-car driveway with an apron is more surface than a single slab.
  • How much buildup. Light film washes fast; years of algae and moss staining take slower passes.
  • Which surfaces. Driveways, walkways, patios, steps and entryways each add area, and each is priced on its own size — one visit can cover several of them at once.

HomeBlue's Seattle pricing is lower, about $0.70–$1.20 per linear foot. By stories, Angi's Seattle figures run about $157–$200 for a one-story home, $188–$275 for two stories, and $234–$311 for three.

Most homes do not know their gutter length offhand, which is normal. It gets measured as part of the estimate, along with how full the gutters actually are.

What moves the price

  • Linear feet of gutter. Bigger roofline, more gutter, more time.
  • Stories. A second or third story means taller ladders and slower, more careful work.
  • How full they are. A season of leaves is one thing; packed, sprouting gutters that have not been touched in years are another.
  • Downspouts. A downspout flow check is included. A clogged downspout that needs to come apart takes extra time.

Comparing quotes

How to compare two quotes

Two quotes for the same house can be far apart and both be honest, because companies count differently. One counts panes, another counts windows. One includes screens and sills, another adds them later. One price is exterior only and the other is inside and out. Before you compare the totals, compare what each quote actually includes.

It also helps to compare the basis. A per-window quote and a whole-house quote are hard to line up until both describe the same work. Ask each company to say, in plain terms, what gets cleaned, what is included, and whether the price is final or an estimate that can move once work starts.

One Way's way is simple: the price is set before any work starts, and frames, sills and screens are included in a standard window clean. If a lower quote beats it on the same work, that is a fair choice. What we would not do is win the job with a number and then add to it at your door.

Questions

Pricing questions

How much does window cleaning cost?

Published figures for this area put a typical whole-home window clean at about $220 (Angi, 2026 cost data), an average Seattle job at $165–$180 (Thumbtack), and a standard window in Everett at $9–$11 (HomeBlue). Your exact price depends on your home, and the estimate is free.

Why don't you list your prices?

Because a price list would be a guess dressed up as a promise. Homes vary too much: window counts, stories, access and condition all change the job. We would rather give you one exact price for your home than a list price that moves when we arrive.

Is the estimate really free?

Yes. The estimate costs nothing. You get the exact price first and decide with the number in front of you.

What does the price include?

A standard window clean includes the glass inside and out, or exterior only if that is what you book, plus the frames, sills and screens. Gutter cleaning includes a downspout flow check. If something is not included, it gets said up front, not discovered later.

Can I get one price for windows, gutters and the driveway together?

Yes. One estimate can cover several jobs, each priced on its own factors, so you see what each part costs and can drop or add pieces before deciding. One visit can cover them all together.

Does the price ever change after work starts?

The price is set before any work starts. The one honest exception is hard water: if a test spot shows the glass is etched rather than spotted, that gets discussed before the rest of the work goes ahead, not after.
